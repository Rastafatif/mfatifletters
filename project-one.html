Modeling of the office building with centralized cooling system

In medium to large buildings, the cooling system design often utilizes a central system, where chillers provide chilled water to the indoor units to cool the space. In this study, I conducted an energy audit for a 7,000-sqm office building and compared the results from the simulated model to the real electricity bill.

From the validated result, I successfully developed an accurate model to use in quantifying the energy saving of energy efficiency measures.

1,272 → 1,256MWh per year

30–40%Less pump load with VSD

Interesting findings

  • Replacing all split-type ACs (some floors are equipped with a decentralized system) with higher-COP units and upgrading tinted glazing to triple low-e will save 16 MWh of annual consumption — from 1,272 MWh to 1,256 MWh, or approximately 70,000 Baht per year.
  • For a large office building, a small improvement in auxiliary systems such as the chilled water pump — replacing a constant speed pump with a Variable Speed Drive (VSD) — can reduce pump load by 30–40%.
  • However, the payback period of retrofitting measures needs to be quantified against the reduced energy cost from the improvement.

Modeling a building in operation

Modeling a system in operation is generally more challenging than modeling at the design development stage. For best accuracy, measurement tools such as flow rate meters, ammeters or data loggers will be needed to collect all the important parameters that influence the energy use of the building.
